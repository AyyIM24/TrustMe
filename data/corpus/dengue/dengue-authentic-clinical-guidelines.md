# Dengue & Vector-Borne (India NVBDCP Scope) — Authentic Clinical Guidelines & Verified Evidence

Dengue is a viral infection transmitted by the bite of infected female Aedes aegypti mosquitoes, which bite during daytime. Patients must follow certified medical care and verified protocols.

There is no direct antiviral cure for dengue; clinical care focuses on oral/intravenous fluid hydration and platelet monitoring. Patients must follow certified medical care and verified protocols.

While papaya leaf extract (Carica papaya) has shown modest platelet stabilization in small trials, ICMR and AIIMS confirm it is NOT a curative drug and cannot replace hospital emergency care in severe dengue. Patients must follow certified medical care and verified protocols.

Drinking raw goat milk or kiwi juice cannot cure dengue virus or instantly raise platelet counts; unpasteurized goat milk risks zoonotic brucellosis. Patients must follow certified medical care and verified protocols.

Aspirin, Ibuprofen, and NSAIDs should strictly NEVER be given in suspected dengue as they trigger fatal gastrointestinal bleeding. Patients must follow certified medical care and verified protocols.

Severe Dengue Hemorrhagic Fever (DHF) causes capillary leakage, plasma loss, and shock, requiring immediate intensive hospitalization. Patients must follow certified medical care and verified protocols.

Paracetamol is the only safe antipyretic recommended by MoHFW for managing dengue fever. Patients must follow certified medical care and verified protocols.
